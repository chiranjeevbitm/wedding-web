## 2 · What we are really building (framing decision)

The original request was *"a very beautiful but very light React-only frontend app for my brother's marriage."* Read against all three sources, that sentence hides **three different products** competing for the same screen:

1. a **wedding website** (invitation-flavoured landing page) — *rejected as the core*
2. an **admin dashboard** (dense tables, filters, Gantt-ish task manager) — *rejected as the tone*
3. a **family-season command centre + noticeboard + keepsake** — *chosen*

### Why (3)

- **The data is not one event.** It is three events across 31 days — Chhath (13–16 Nov), the maternal cousin's wedding (27 Nov → ~3 Dec), and Deepak's wedding (7–13 Dec) — with the *same* people travelling, shopping, fasting, arranging and recovering in between. A single-event wedding site structurally cannot express the `3 Dec → 7 Dec` turnaround, which is the hardest and most emotional part of the whole season.
- **The audience is not "guests."** It is roughly 20–60 relatives who need *"what's happening, when, what do I bring, where do I stay"* — plus one power user (the user) who needs the full checklist. An invitation site serves neither.
- **The material is not disposable.** It contains elders' recollections of how rituals are done in this family, folk songs, prasad, ghat logistics. That has archival value after 13 Dec 2026. So the same codebase ships a **Memories mode**.

### The three-layer mental model the site must express

```
FAMILY JOURNEY   →   CALENDAR   →   EACH DAY   →   TASK / ITEM / PERSON / RITUAL
 (the story)        (the map)      (the room)        (the details)
```

Every feature must be traceable to one of those four layers. Features that fit none of them are out of scope.

### The two audiences we deliberately do NOT design for in v1

- **Public visitors / the internet at large.** The site contains family travel plans, addresses, phone-level logistics. It ships with `noindex` and an optional passcode (see `wedding-website-plan.md` §17.4).

### 4.2 A genuinely useful discovery about 11 December

The independent vivah-muhurat listing places the shubh window for **11 Dec 2026 (Tritiya, Uttara Ashadha, Shukla paksha)** as an **overnight window** — the listing renders the shubh samay as `03:05 – 07:04` referencing the small hours. Two consequences:

1. **It corroborates the transcript's own logic** ("बारात आरीक बहुत हैं… बारात से आने में" → the baraat comes back very late on the night of the 11th). A late-night or pre-dawn vivah muhurat is entirely consistent with why the family pushed the reception to Sunday.
2. **The app must never hard-code a muhurat.** The correct design is:
   - show `11 Dec 2026 · शुक्रवार` and a **VERIFIED: shubh date** chip (with the panchang note "Tritiya · Uttara Ashadha")
   - show an **empty, edit-controlled field** labelled `VIVAH MUHURAT — enter confirmed time from the family pandit`, with source = "Family Pandit"
   - show a small note: *"Muhurat is location-specific and must be confirmed for Muzaffarpur."*

### 4.3 Also worth noting: Kharmas and Chaturmas

- **Chaturmas 2026:** ~25 Jul → 20 Nov 2026 (Devshayani Ekadashi → Prabodhini Ekadashi). This is exactly *why* late November and December are the marriage window in 2026 — it is also why the cousin's wedding sits at 27 Nov and not earlier.
- **Kharmas 2026–27:** begins **16 Dec 2026** (Surya enters Dhanu) and runs to **14 Jan 2027**. Nothing shubh happens then — which is why 13 Dec reception is the season's natural closing date, and why the "after the wedding, everyone scatters" pattern in the transcript makes sense.
- Practical implication for the app: the **Journey** screen can honestly say *"the family has a 27-day window between Chhath and Kharmas — and we are using all of it."*

**v1.1 (correct):**

### 5.7 · The shopping-availability layer (a genuinely new feature)

The real constraint is not which day is free — it is **when the working members of the family are physically in Muzaffarpur and free to shop.** So the app gains a fourth lens on the same data:

| **7–8 Nov** (Sat–Sun) | ✅ free — but it is Diwali week | 🟢 Early | Bulk purchases, clothes, gifts, non-perishables. **Buying during Diwali week means crowds and higher prices — go early or avoid the 8th** |

> **Buy early → store at home → travel to Delhi → return → only final/emergency purchases → the wedding begins.**

### 6.2 Three structural insights that shape the whole product

**(a) The three events are of different *kinds*, so they need different UI.**

| Track | Kind of event | Primary need | Implication |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Home base (`home`)** | The backdrop: six weeks in one city, one household, everything from Diwali to reception | *"Where is everyone right now?"*, *"when is anyone free?"* | Needs a **presence layer** — people, availability, shopping windows — not just events |
| Chhath | Devotional festival, household-wide, fasting, ghat logistics | Prasad, soop/daura, ghat plan, who fasts, sunrise/sunset | Needs a **soop/ghat/prasad module**, not a wedding checklist |
| Cousin's wedding | **A home-base matkor (27 Nov) + a four-day Delhi excursion (30 Nov – 3 Dec)** | Travel legs, who's away, packing, tickets, stay in Delhi | Needs **travel & stay primacy** — but scoped to one excursion, not a whole track |
| Deepak's wedding | The main wedding: 7 functions, performances, host duty, in the home | Everything: checklist, people, music, photography, vendors | Needs the **full day-sheet treatment** |

**(b) The 3 → 7 December gap is still the season's biggest risk — but for a different reason.**

> **v1.1 correction:** the transcript never explicitly said "office on the 3rd". What it did say — *"तीन तारीक के आफ़िस करके रात में रिटर्निंग"* — now reads unambiguously as **Delhi → Muzaffarpur on the night of 3 December.** The family is not arriving from far away on an unknown date; **four people come home on Thursday, and the wedding week starts on Monday.** That is a four-day gap, not a crisis — but the reasoning below still holds, sharpened.

The risk was never "not enough days". It is that:

- **30 Nov – 3 Dec is a whole week the home base is not preparing** — nobody is in the city to receive the shopping, accept deliveries, or set up the house while the matriarchs are away;
- **5–6 December are the only two clean days** after the return, and 5 Dec must absorb **both** the final shopping and the unpacking;
- **the geet cycle starts Monday 7 Dec** and needs rehearsal time in a house that has just been set up.

So this gap is promoted to a **first-class object** in the app: a "crunch" callout on Journey, a prep-checklist spanning 4–6 Dec, and a **"who's home to receive deliveries?"** row on the Travel screen.

**(c) Geet is not Sangeet.**

This is a genuine modelling insight, not a naming nicety:

| | **गीत / Geet** | **संगीत / Sangeet** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What | Traditional ceremonial folk songs, sung mostly by the women of the family | A structured performance event — stage, sound, rehearsed items |
| Recurrence | **Repeats across days** (7, 8, 9 Dec; also around the wedding day in some families) | **One night (or one block)**, 10 Dec |
| Context | Happens in the house, alongside the ritual | Happens as an organised function |
| Data need | Songs, who sings, lyrics, meaning, when sung | Performances, order, props, sound, rehearsal time |

Both source drafts also treat Geet as a **five-day ritual cycle** ("गीत पांच दिन… शादी वाला दिन लगा के पांच दिन"). So in the data model, `geet` is an **attribute of a day** (a flag + song references), while `sangeet` is **an event**. Getting this wrong would flatten the family's most important musical tradition into a single playlist.

**(d) Chhath's timing mirrors the wedding's timing.**

Chhath is anchored to the sun (sunset arghya, sunrise arghya); the wedding is anchored to the pandit (muhurat, baraat). Both mean: **the app must never present a time as fixed when it is astronomically or ritually variable.** Every such time gets an editable field plus a "who decides" label (Panchang / Pandit / Family).

## 7 · Chhath deep dive (Track A)

### 7.1 What Chhath actually is, in one paragraph

Chhath (छठ; also *Chhath Parva*, *Dala Chhath*, *Surya Shashthi*) is an ancient four-day festival native to Bihar, Jharkhand, eastern Uttar Pradesh and the Madhesh/Koshi/Gandaki belt of Nepal, observed by Maithils, Bhojpuriyas, Magahis and Tharus. It venerates **Surya** and **Chhathi Maiya (Shashthi)** and is unusual among Hindu festivals in that it needs **no priest and no temple** — the devotee offers directly to the sun, standing in water. Wikipedia's 2026 entry places the festival on **13–16 November 2026**, matching the panchang sequence used in both source drafts.

Structural facts that matter for the app:

- The fast is kept by a **vratin** (the observant). Everyone else supports: cooking, carrying, escorting, arranging the ghat.
- The **~36-hour nirjala fast** runs from after Kharna (evening of 14 Nov) through Sandhya Arghya (15 Nov) to Usha Arghya (16 Nov morning).
- **Arghya times are astronomical, not administrative** — local sunset on the 15th, local sunrise on the 16th. So the app must show a *"local time — confirm for Muzaffarpur"* label, an editable field, and a panchang link. Hard-coding "5:12 pm" would be both wrong and disrespectful.
- Cold mid-November river mornings, elderly vratins and crowded ghats make **warmth and safety legitimate checklist items**, not padding.

### 7.2 The four days, as the app should present them

| Day | Hindi | Roman | What the household does | The app's job |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 · 13 Nov (Fri) | नहाय-खाय | Nahay Khay | Ritual bath; sattvik meal (kaddu-bhaat, chana dal + rice, arwa chawal). Cooking is done with special care; the house is cleaned thoroughly | "House cleaning" + "sattvik menu" checklist; mark the day the fasting regime begins |
| 2 · 14 Nov (Sat) | खरना / लोहंडा | Kharna / Lohanda | Day-long fast; in the evening **gud ki kheer + roti** (prasad) is made and distributed; the nirjala fast begins after this | Prasad quantities, who cooks, whom to distribute to, plus a prominent "36-hour fast starts tonight" banner |
| 3 · 15 Nov (Sun) | संध्या अर्घ्य | Sandhya Arghya | Family reaches the ghat before sunset; the vratin stands in water and offers arghya with soop/daura; folk songs are sung | Ghat plan, departure time, who carries the daura, parking, meeting point, prasad transport, **what to wear for the cold** |
| 4 · 16 Nov (Mon) | उषा अर्घ्य + पारण | Usha Arghya + Parana | Before sunrise at the same ghat; arghya to the rising sun; then **parana** — the fast breaks with prasad, ginger and water | Leave/absence flag (the user takes leave), breakfast/prasad, then the **handoff to travel-to-Muzaffarpur** |

### 7.3 The Muzaffarpur-specific layer (and how to keep it honest)

Chhath in Muzaffarpur is a river-and-pokhar city event. The district is on the **Budhi Gandak / Bagmati belt** of North Bihar with a dense network of ghats and pokhars, and local administration coverage repeatedly describes large Chhath gatherings with cleaning, barricading, lighting, medical support, crowd control and traffic diversion. Commonly referenced city locations include river-ghat stretches such as **Sidhighat / Akharaghat / Ashram Ghat** and pond sites such as **Sahu Pokhar**.

The design response is a **Ghat Plan form**, empty by default:

```
छठ · घाट की योजना
────────────────────────────────
घाट (Ghat)              [ परिवार का घाट ▾ ]   ← `toVerify`, empty in seed data
कौन दौरा/सूप ले जाएगा    [ व्यक्ति ▾ ]
पार्किंग                [ जगह जोड़ें ]
मिलने की जगह             [ जगह जोड़ें ]
कितने लोग जा रहे हैं      [ संख्या ]
सुबह निकलने का समय       [ समय ]
सूर्यास्त / सूर्योदय      [ स्थान से, सम्पादन-योग्य ]
आने-जाने का साधन          [ गाड़ी / ई-रिक्शा / … ]
```

Two laws applied here:

1. **Never name a specific ghat in the seed data.** Naming "Sidhighat" would be us inventing the family's tradition.
2. **Do show a city reference list** — but inside a collapsible "परंपरा / context" block clearly labelled as general city information, not family fact.

### 7.4 Prasad & the soop inventory (the most error-prone checklist in the season)

Traditional soop/daura contents, assembled from Chhath practice sources and Bihar Tourism's description (which highlights kheer, fruit and prasad):

| Category | Items | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Soop / daura** | soop (bamboo winnow), daura/dala, bamboo basket, sup | Often handed down through generations; sometimes bought new |
| **Sweets** | thekua (wheat, jaggery, ghee), gud ki kheer, pua, arsa | Thekua is the signature prasad; quantities are large |
| **Fruits** | banana (whole bunch), sugarcane (full canes), coconut, apple, orange, papaya, seasonal fruit, dry fruits | Must be whole and unblemished |
| **Roots/vegetables** | ginger (adrak), turmeric (haldi), radish, sweet potato | The "kosi" offering traditionally includes sugarcane and lamps |
| **Offerings** | sindoor, akshat, supari, paan, elaichi, laung, dhoop/agarbatti, diya + oil/ghee, camphor, matchbox | Small items, fatal if forgotten |
| **Light** | clay diyas (many), lantern, matches, spare wicks | Evening arghya needs a lot of lamps |
| **Kitchen** | jaggery, wheat flour, ghee, chana, rice, milk, sugar | For kheer and thekua |
| **Ghat kit** | mat, blanket for the vratin, warm clothes, towel, torch, drinking water, medicines, first-aid, thermos tea | Mid-November river mornings are cold |

Ship this as a **"प्रसाद किट"** template: one tap inserts the whole set into the 14–16 Nov checklist, fully editable and deletable.

### 7.5 Why Chhath gets its own module rather than three calendar rows

- Its checklist semantics are about **offerings and vessels**, not tasks and vendors.
- Its icon vocabulary is **objects** (soop, daura, sugarcane, coconut, diya, water, sun) — nothing like the wedding's (mehndi cones, sherwani, horse, band).
- Its "why" is devotional and household-wide; the wedding's is ritualistic and couple-centric.
- Its primary audience is the **mother/elders layer** and it must be fully usable **in Hindi alone**.

┌────────────────────────────────────────────────┐

│  27 नवंबर – 3 दिसंबर 2026        [ ~ to verify ] │

│  ~ 3 दिसं (रात)      ऑफ़िस + वापसी                │
│                                                │
│  [+ तारीख़ भरें]  ← किसी भी '?' को ठीक करें       │

**Rules for cluster rendering:**

1. Unknown interior days render as a **single grey band** with the label "तारीख़ तय नहीं", never as a sequence of empty days.

3. The cluster contributes to the Journey timeline as one node with a `~`, so the season's shape is still honest.
4. The cluster's travel rows flow into **Travel & Stay** even while dates are unknown (mode, people, luggage are knowable independently).

### 11.2 The "Bajjika, not Madhubani" correction — and why it changes the design

Both source drafts reached first for **Madhubani/Mithila** aesthetics as the visual identity. That is a **cultural mismatch worth correcting politely**:

- **Madhubani painting belongs to Mithila** — the Maithili-speaking belt (Darbhanga, Madhubani, Sitamarhi, the Janakpur side of the border).
- **Muzaffarpur is officially associated with the Bajjika cultural zone**, a distinct zone in Bihar's own cultural administration.
- The two are neighbours and share motifs, but conflating them is exactly the kind of genericisation this family would notice.

**Resulting design position:**

> **Muzaffarpur + Bajjika/North-Bihar family culture first**, with **Mithila-style line work used as an influence** (folk linework, lotus, fish, bamboo/basket forms) — never labelled "Madhubani", and never treated as evidence of what this family does.

| Generic-Madhubani choice | Bajjika / North-Bihar-appropriate choice |
| --- | --- |
| Declaring a "Madhubani wedding" | "मुज़फ़्फ़रपुर · Tirhut · North Bihar" |
| Large painted Madhubani panels | **Folk border linework** + **bamboo/sikki basket** shapes |
| Mithila-specific deity panels | **Soop, daura, diya, sun, marigold, banana leaf** |
| Madhubani fish in every corner | Fish/lotus as **small repeating border motifs** (shared regional vocabulary) |

### 11.4 Matkor — the full `context` entry (the most important one)

| Aspect | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Also called | मटकोर · मटकोड़ · मड़वा मटकोर |
| Meaning | From *"मिट्टी खोदना"* — digging earth |
| When | **2–3 days before the wedding** |
| Who | Women of the **groom's and the bride's** household; the digging is done by the groom's/bride's **bua (aunt) or sister** |
| What they carry | जौ (barley) · पान (paan) · पीला कपड़ा (yellow cloth) |
| What happens there | मंगल गीत are sung · the **phawra (spade) is worshipped with sindoor** · the soil is dug · **five women's laps (खोईंछा) are filled** |
| What happens after | Soil is carried home in a **दउरी** · a **कलश is established** after the pandit's puja using that soil · then the **haldi** of the groom/bride · then a **कंगन / ककन** (iron ring + thread) is tied on the wrist |
| Songs | Matkor geet form a recognised genre in Bhojpuri and Maithili folk song. A widely documented one is *"कहवां के पियर माटी, कहवां के कुदार हे…"* |
| Scholarship | Kuber Nath Rai treated matkor as originally a **tribal** marriage practice surviving in various forms; matkor appears today even in devotional celebrations of Shiv–Parvati and Ram–Sita weddings |
| **Variability warning** | Published sources explicitly note that Bihar wedding rituals vary across communities and that modernisation and regional diversity make a single universal sequence impossible |

**Therefore the app must say "हमारे घर का मटकोर" — never "बिहार का मटकोर".**

### 12.2 Fog — the reason the calendar has a buffer rule

North Bihar winters are fog-prone, and Muzaffarpur specifically sees **multi-hour train delays** in the fog season; Indian Railways routinely announces winter timetable changes affecting services from December. That is not a hypothetical — it is the single most likely way the family's December plan actually breaks.

**Therefore:**

1. The Travel screen carries a **permanent, non-dismissible** winter advisory.
2. Every travel row has `expected` **and** `actual` arrival times.
3. Any function starting within 6 h of an arrival is flagged `🚩 tight`.
4. The **3 → 7 Dec** window is labelled the **crunch** and given its own prep checklist.
5. The app recommends booking early and adding a buffer, and links the [mode] note: *train is generally the most robust*, but nothing is guaranteed in fog.

### 12.3 Booking-window reality check (a genuine planning risk)

Indian Railways' **Advance Reservation Period (ARP)** has been published as **60 days**, with 2026 reporting also discussing extended windows for Aadhaar-authenticated users. This is a fast-moving rule and must be **verified on IRCTC at the time of booking** — the app should say so rather than assert a number.

Consequence for this plan: by the plan date (29 Sep 2026), travel for **13–16 Nov**, **27 Nov** and **3 Dec** is inside or near the booking window, and **5–13 Dec** is around the edge of a 60-day window. So the app's travel screen should carry an explicit **"book now"** prompt with the date list rather than pretending there is time.

## 14 · Data model & status taxonomy (the authoritative schema)

### 14.1 The status taxonomy (used on every fact in the app)

| Status | Meaning | How it was decided | UI chip |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `confirmed` | Settled: written schedule, verified calendar, or explicitly resolved in the family conversation | S1 / S5 / transcript with corroboration | solid, no qualifier |
| `tentative` | Discussed and provisionally agreed, but not finalised | transcript, single-line | outlined + dotted underline + "~" |
| `toVerify` | Mentioned but never settled; or entirely unknown | transcript gap / absent from all sources | amber "? to confirm" |
| `suggested` | **Our** planning scaffolding. Not a family claim | us | grey italic "planning suggestion" |
| `context` | General cultural/regional background | published sources | muted, "Traditionally…" prefix, always with a disclaimer |

Additional orthogonal flags, applied independently of status:

| Flag | Applies to | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `morning` / `evening` / `day` / `night` | event | time-of-day bucket when no exact time exists |
| `geetDay` | event | this day includes गीत |
| `outdoor` | event | triggers the winter note + winter kit |
| `needsPandit` | slot | the time/value is decided by the pandit |
| `needsPanchang` | slot | the time/value is astronomical (arghya) |
| `userLeave` | event | a leave-from-work flag (16 Nov, 3 Dec, possibly 11–14 Dec) |

### 15.6c How to update this seed (maintenance)

| Situation | What to change | What happens in the app |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A date is confirmed | Flip `status` from `toVerify`/`tentative` to `confirmed` in `events.js` or `clusters.js`, bump the seed version | The chip changes everywhere; the Decisions Log row flips; the calendar dot solidifies |
| A new event appears | Add an `Event` object with a unique `id` and a track | It appears in the calendar, Journey and Day Sheet automatically |
| A ritual's family practice is learned | Fill `familyText` in `rituals.js` — **never** touch `contextText` | The "Why" tab shows both blocks side by side, correctly attributed |
| The family rejects one of our suggestions | Delete that row from `bridge` events or mark its `status:'suggested'` items deleted | Our scaffolding disappears; our opinions were never load-bearing |
| A kit is wrong for this family | Edit the kit's `items[]` — or delete the kit | Future inserts change; already-inserted items are untouched family data |

**Three things that must never be edited into the seed:**
1. A person's name, phone number or address.
2. A `familyText` value (that belongs to the family, at runtime).
3. `CHHATH_GHAT.ghat`, or any `slots[].time` for a pandit- or panchang-decided slot.

### 21.5 How these questions should be *asked*

Not as a form. The app already has a **Decisions Log** and a **Questions** list with an `owner` field. The practical approach is:

1. The user asks 3–4 questions per phone call, no more.
2. Each answer is typed into the app once, and the row flips from `?` to `✓`.
3. The calendar, Journey and Day Sheet update automatically.
4. Nobody has to remember the answer, because it is written down in the place where it will be needed.

### 22.3 Maintenance principles

- **Seed changes are small commits.** One fact per commit, with the source in the message (`seed: matkor ghat confirmed — per Mummy, call 12 Oct`).
- **Never delete a `context` entry** because the family disagrees with it — instead, fill the `familyText` block beside it. The disagreement *is* the archive.
- **Never let the app state a fact it cannot source.** If a new date enters the seed, its `status` must be one of the five values, and if it is `confirmed`, the source must be nameable in the commit message.
- **Keep the two documents in sync.** A change in the seed that contradicts the dossier is a bug in the dossier; fix the dossier in the same commit.
- **After 14 Dec 2026**, freeze the seed, switch to Memories mode, and treat the whole thing as an archive rather than a plan.
